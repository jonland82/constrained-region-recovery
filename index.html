Constrained Region Recovery

What can samples and distances reveal about a hidden output constraint?

Jonathan R. Landers

The question

An original distribution \(P_0\) is restricted to an unknown region \(B\). We observe samples from the restricted law and ask what those samples—and their distances—can tell us about \(B\).

\[Q(A)=\frac{P_0(A\cap B)}{P_0(B)}.\]

02 / Shared references

Can shared references sharpen region recovery?

Distances to common anchors place samples in a shared space. A fixed candidate family then gives finite-sample error bounds, with a two-sample experiment.

Read HTML Read PDF

What the results show

  • Distances within a constrained sample may leave the region and its original mass unidentified.
  • Shared anchors resolve location when their coordinates and cross-distances are known; unseen boundaries still require assumptions.
  • On a fixed 625-region grid containing the truth, the shared-reference experiment reached zero median region error with at least 100 constrained samples. Its theoretical bounds were conservative.

These are focused explanatory results; the experiment is not a matched comparison proving one estimator is better.
